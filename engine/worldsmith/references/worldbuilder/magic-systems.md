# Magic Systems

Magic is a system of cause and effect inside a fictional world. The craft is in choosing what is and isn't possible, and making those choices feel earned.

## The hard-to-soft spectrum

Sanderson's framing is useful: how much can the reader/player predict what magic will do?

- **Hard magic** — explicit rules, costs, and limits. Readers can solve problems with it because they understand it (Mistborn, Fullmetal Alchemist, most TTRPGs).
- **Soft magic** — mysterious, awe-driven, often wielded by distant figures. Solves problems only by creating new ones (Gandalf, the Force in the original trilogy, most fairy tales).
- **Hybrid** — common and arguably best. A core set of legible rules with a mysterious frontier (Earthsea, Harry Potter at its best).

**Sanderson's First Law:** the ability to solve problems with magic is proportional to how well the audience understands it. Soft magic should create problems, not solve them.

Ask the user where on the spectrum they want to land before designing mechanics. A hard system needs rules; a soft system needs *texture* — sensory detail, ritual, atmosphere — instead.

## The three questions every magic system must answer

1. **What can it do?** (Capabilities — and the ceiling.)
2. **What does it cost?** (Time, materials, body, sanity, soul, social standing, something irreplaceable.)
3. **What are its limits?** (Hard rules that cannot be broken, regardless of skill.)

If any of these is "anything / nothing / none," the system is broken for narrative purposes. Cost and limit are what make magic *dramatic*. Capability without cost is wish fulfillment, which gets boring fast.

## The originating question

Before mechanics, ask: **where does the magic come from?** This drives almost everything else.

- **Innate** (bloodline, mutation, soul) → magic becomes a class marker; politics of breeding, eugenics, persecution
- **Learned** (study, apprenticeship) → academies, gatekeeping, libraries, heresy
- **Granted** (gods, spirits, contracts) → patrons matter; magic implies religion or pact
- **Earned** (sacrifice, trial, suffering) → barrier to entry creates rarity and respect
- **Environmental** (ley lines, materials, locations) → geography becomes power; resource wars
- **Universal** (everyone can, varying skill) → no magic underclass; society reorganizes around it entirely

Mix these for texture: "innate potential, but requires study to access, and the gods can revoke it."

## The cost ladder (rank these by severity for your system)

- Mundane resources (money, time, materials)
- Physical fatigue
- Injury or permanent bodily change
- Lifespan
- Memory or identity
- Sanity
- A specific moral compromise required to cast
- Something taken from someone else
- The thing being protected by the spell

Higher-cost magic naturally produces interesting choices. If a spell costs nothing, why wouldn't everyone cast it constantly?

## The limit ladder

Hard limits are the rules that *cannot* be broken. They define the shape of the magic and force creative problem-solving:

- Cannot affect specific things (the dead, the named, the kin)
- Cannot exceed a measurable threshold
- Requires conditions (time of day, materials, locations, witnesses)
- Cannot be done alone / can only be done alone
- Cannot be undone
- Cannot be hidden (leaves a trace, mark, smell, sound)

The best limits are the ones that *should* be breakable but aren't — they generate the entire plot when someone tries.

## The ripple checklist

After drafting the mechanics, force these implications. If the user doesn't have answers, those are the next questions:

- **Economics:** Who profits from magic? Who's displaced by it? What jobs exist that wouldn't otherwise? What jobs *don't* exist because magic does them?
- **Politics:** Who controls access? Is there a magical aristocracy, guild, church? What does treason look like in this system?
- **War:** How does magic change battles? Sieges? Assassination? Why hasn't the strongest mage just taken over?
- **Daily life:** Is magic in the kitchen, the field, the bedroom? Or only in the throne room and the temple?
- **Religion:** Is magic divine, profane, neutral? Who decides?
- **Ethics:** What's the magical equivalent of murder? Of theft? Of consent violation?
- **The forbidden:** What kind of magic is taboo, and why? Taboo magic is plot fuel.

## Common pitfalls

- **Power creep.** New spells / capabilities introduced ad hoc to solve problems. Lock in the ceiling early.
- **Free magic.** No cost means no stakes. Even small costs help (a spell that always leaves the caster cold for an hour shapes behavior).
- **Convenient exceptions.** "Magic can't bring back the dead — except this one time." Once a limit is broken, it's not a limit anymore. If you need an exception, *make it canon* and explore the implications.
- **Underexplored implications.** A world where healing magic exists shouldn't have plagues unless you've answered why.
- **Magic without culture.** Spells without rituals, gestures, words, traditions feel sterile. The *practice* matters as much as the mechanic.

## Originality moves

- **Restrict the obvious.** Fire magic exists but can only be cast by people who've forgiven someone who wronged them.
- **Couple two unrelated systems.** Magic that runs on debt. Magic that requires music. Magic that only works while you're being truthful.
- **Localize.** Magic only works in this valley. Or only at this latitude. Or only when this star is up.
- **Make the cost beautiful, not painful.** Magic ages you, but the aging is visible as silver in your hair, and silver hair is a status symbol because it means you helped someone.
- **Magic that wants something.** Treat the magic itself as having preferences — it works better for some intents than others — and the system becomes a character.

## Output format

When drafting a magic system as a finalized doc, use this structure:

```
# [Name of system, if any]

## Source
Where the magic comes from and who has access.

## Mechanics
How it's cast. Words, gestures, materials, time, conditions.

## Costs
What it takes from the caster. Be specific.

## Limits
What it cannot do, no matter what.

## Cultural footprint
How the world has organized itself around this system.
Education, professions, taboos, laws, religion.

## Open questions
Threads left for later.
```

For soft-magic systems, replace "Mechanics" and "Limits" with "Manifestations" (what it looks/feels like when it happens) and "Mysteries" (what no one understands).

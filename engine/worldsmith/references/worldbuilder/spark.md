# Spark: the creative engine

The inventor's toolkit. Read it when an idea feels generic, the user is stuck,
a round of options needs to be better, or a domain is about to be drafted.

## Contents

2. Generative techniques
3. Making options worth choosing

## 1. The voice

The collaborator is a fellow builder who is visibly enjoying the work and has
read widely. That shows in what gets said, not in exclamation marks.

- **Lead with the implication.** Open on the most interesting consequence of
  what the user just decided, not on a summary of it.
- **Concrete first.** One vivid particular (a coin, a law, a smell, a ruin)
  beats three abstract sentences about culture.
- **Bring a case.** "This is how the Venetians did it" or "Ibn Khaldun would
  say this dynasty has three generations left" makes an idea click and gives it
  weight. Keep it to a phrase or a sentence; this is seasoning.
- **Disagree when it helps.** If an idea is weaker than the user's best work,
  say so and offer the stronger version. A collaborator who likes everything
  is useless.
- **End with a hook, not a menu.** Close a turn on one concrete next move or
  one charged question, not a list of everything that could happen next.

## 2. Generative techniques

**The third-idea rule.** The first idea is the genre default. The second is
its obvious inversion. Discard both silently and pitch the third, which
usually comes from asking what the first two have in common and breaking that.
Dragons hoard gold (default). Dragons hoard memories (inversion, now itself a
cliché). Third: dragons are how the kingdom does accounting; a dragon's hoard
is the treasury's audit trail, and killing one erases the debts recorded in it.

**Collision.** Put two unrelated real things in the same room and keep only
what survives the impact. Monastic scriptoria plus high-frequency trading. The
Hanseatic League plus a fungal ecology. The collision must change both sides.

**Follow one constraint all the way down.** Pick a single rule and derive
everything from it before adding anything else. "Light travels slowly here,
about walking pace." Then: architecture faces the past, news is literally old,
astronomy is archaeology, warfare is about predicting where an enemy was.

**The view from below.** Re-describe the setting as a tax collector, a
midwife, a ferryman, a prisoner, a child would see it. The grand system
becomes real only where it touches a person who did not design it.

**The cost and the exception.** Every power has a price someone pays, and
every rule has one famous exception everyone argues about. Name both. The
exception is usually where the first story lives.

**Scale shift.** Take a domestic thing to the scale of an empire (a family
feud that is also a civil war) or an imperial thing down to a household (a
church schism fought over one kitchen's rules).

**The residue test.** Ask what this idea leaves behind after three centuries:
in place names, slang, ruins, superstitions, laws nobody remembers the reason
for. If it leaves nothing, it is not yet connected to the world.

**Steal the mechanism, change the surface.** Take a real precedent from
`references/worldbuilder/precedents.md`, keep the mechanism that made it work, and replace
every surface detail. The Venetian doge election (lot alternating with vote to
defeat factions) becomes a starship captaincy chosen by alternating random
crew draws and ballots, because the ship's founders feared mutiny cliques.

**Ask what is missing.** List the five things every genre setting has, then
remove one and follow the consequences. No horses. No writing. No night. No
inheritance.

## 3. Making options worth choosing

When offering choices in an interview round or a draft:

- **Three options, three directions.** Not small, medium, large of one idea.
  Each should take the world somewhere different.
- **Each option carries a payoff line.** What it unlocks for story or play,
  in a clause.
- **At least one is grounded, at least one is strange.** The grounded one
  cites a precedent; the strange one is the third idea.
- **Never pad.** Two strong options beat three with a filler.

Example, for "how is the ruler chosen?":
1. *Open contest among kin, as in Gaelic tanistry.* Every succession is a
   campaign season; cousins are both heirs and threats.
2. *The ruler is whoever holds the archive key; the key is hidden at each
   death.* Succession is a treasure hunt, and archivists are kingmakers.
3. *The throne passes to whoever the old ruler's grief chose.* Legitimacy is
   a matter of who was loved, which makes deathbed access the most valuable
   currency in the court.

## 4. Energy over a long build

- **Alternate scale.** After a structural round, zoom into one vivid
  particular; after a detail session, zoom back out to what it implies.
- **Pay off earlier choices.** When a new detail connects to something the
  user decided sessions ago, point it out. Coherence is its own delight.
- **Keep a short list of "loose threads that excite"** in `00-overview.md`
  open questions. Offer one when momentum dips, rather than asking what to do
  next.
- **Let the user lead when they are hot.** If they are pouring out ideas,
  capture and connect; do not interrupt with structure.
- **Small finished things.** A one-paragraph folk saying, a single law with
  its punishment, a coin's inscription. Finishing something small keeps a
  large build feeling alive.

## 5. Touchstones by register

Use these as shared vocabulary for tone and as examples of a technique done
well. Mention one when it sharpens a conversation; never as homework.

| Register | Touchstones | What they teach |
|---|---|---|
| Ecological and political epic | Herbert, *Dune* | ecology drives religion drives politics |
| Anthropological | Le Guin, *The Dispossessed*, *Earthsea* | build the society through one honest outsider's eye |
| Geology as oppression | Jemisin, *The Broken Earth* | a physical rule that is also a social caste system |
| Deep time, unreliable record | Wolfe, *The Book of the New Sun* | a far future that reads as the past; the narrator lies by omission |
| Language first | Tolkien | names and languages carry the history before the plot does |
| Ritual and decay | Peake, *Gormenghast* | a culture of rules that nobody understands anymore |
| Lightly fantasized history | Guy Gavriel Kay, *The Sarantine Mosaic* | the texture of Byzantium without its names |
| Imperial succession and legitimacy | Martine, *A Memory Called Empire*; Leckie, *Ancillary Justice* | empire as language, memory and contested personhood |
| Post-scarcity politics | Banks, the Culture novels | what conflict looks like when material need is solved |
| Future as Enlightenment | Ada Palmer, *Terra Ignota* | a future society written in an eighteenth-century voice |
| Weird city | Miéville, *Perdido Street Station* | density, grime and many species in one economy |
| Planetary engineering | Kim Stanley Robinson, the Mars trilogy | terraforming as a political argument |
| History as science | Asimov, *Foundation* | societies modeled at scale, individuals as perturbations |
| Archaeologist's sweep | Erikson, *Malazan Book of the Fallen* | layers of dead civilizations under every battle |
| Grimdark ground level | Glen Cook, *The Black Company*; Abercrombie | soldiers' view, competence, moral cost |

Non-fiction that feeds worldbuilders well: Fernand Braudel on the longue
durée; Ibn Khaldun's *Muqaddimah* on group cohesion and dynastic cycles; James
C. Scott, *Seeing Like a State* and *Against the Grain*; Graeber and Wengrow,
*The Dawn of Everything*; Peter Turchin on elite overproduction; Jared Diamond,
*Guns, Germs, and Steel* (stimulating, and contested by many historians; say so
if it comes up).

## 6. Anti-patterns

- **Novelty for its own sake.** Strange with no consequences is noise. Every
  invention must change something else in the world.
- **The kitchen sink.** Five cool ideas in one culture cancel each other out.
  One dominant idea, followed deeply, reads as more original than five.
- **Earth with the serial numbers filed off.** "Fantasy Vikings" transplanted
  whole. Borrow mechanisms, not packages.
- **Pitching the same shape every time.** Vary the kinds of option offered:
  sometimes a law, sometimes a ritual, sometimes a material, sometimes a
  person.
- **Hype in place of content.** "This is going to be amazing" adds nothing.
  The detail that makes it amazing does.

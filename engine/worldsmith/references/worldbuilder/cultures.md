# Cultures & Societies

A culture is how a group of people answers the recurring questions of being alive together: how to eat, raise children, marry, die, treat strangers, divide labor, settle disputes, mark time. Build a few of these answers in specific detail and the rest can be sketched.

## The originating constraint

Cultures don't arise in a vacuum. Anchor a culture in *something*:

- **Geography:** A people of the high steppes raise different food, fight different wars, worship different gods than a delta people.
- **A defining event:** A culture shaped by surviving a plague, an exile, a betrayal, a long peace.
- **A material constraint:** Scarce wood, abundant salt, no iron, a single river, perpetual snow.
- **A neighbor:** Cultures are often defined against an "other" — the empire next door, the people they conquered, the ones who conquered them.
- **A core belief:** A society organized around ancestor worship looks fundamentally different from one organized around martial honor.

Pick one or two and let everything else cascade from them. This is the antidote to "culture as costume" — a coat of paint over generic medieval Europe.

## The texture layers

A culture comes alive in specifics across these layers. Don't build all of them every time; pick the ones that matter for the story.

### Material life
- **Food:** What's the daily meal? The feast meal? What's taboo? What's status? Who cooks?
- **Clothing:** Daily, formal, ritual. What signals class, age, marital status, profession?
- **Shelter:** What does a typical house look like? A noble's? A worker's? Who lives with whom?
- **Tools & objects:** What does a person carry on them? What's the most precious household object?

### Social structure
- **Family:** Nuclear, extended, clan, matrilineal, patrilineal, fluid? Who inherits?
- **Marriage:** Who chooses, who pays, who joins whom? Monogamous, polygamous, serial?
- **Children:** When does childhood end? What rite marks adulthood? Who raises them?
- **Elders:** Honored, dreaded, ignored? What's their role?
- **Class:** How many tiers? How rigid? Movement possible?
- **Outsiders:** How are strangers treated? Refugees? Conquered peoples? Other races/species?

### Values & ethics
- **Highest virtue:** Honor? Wisdom? Loyalty? Kindness? Cunning? Industriousness?
- **Cardinal sin:** Cowardice? Greed? Disloyalty? Sacrilege? Idleness?
- **Honor system:** Personal, familial, communal? What restores honor lost?
- **Death & grief:** What's a good death? How are the dead handled? Mourning duration and form?
- **Time orientation:** Do they look back (golden age past), forward (progress), or down (eternal present)?

### Daily texture
- **Greetings:** What's the first thing strangers say to each other? Friends? Lovers? Enemies?
- **Calendar:** How is the year divided? What's a workday vs. rest day? Major festivals?
- **Games & arts:** What do people do for fun? What do children play? What do adults watch?
- **Sex & sexuality:** What's normative, what's tolerated, what's taboo? Who's allowed what?
- **Hospitality:** What does it mean to enter someone's home? To break bread? To refuse a gift?

### Conflict & justice
- **Disputes:** How are they settled? Elder, court, duel, vote, divination?
- **Crime:** What's the worst? What's the lightest? What kinds of crime are even *legible* here?
- **Punishment:** Death, exile, restitution, shame, mutilation, labor?
- **War:** Who fights, why, how? What's a warrior's social position?

## The folk vs. elite split

Almost every culture has an elite version (what nobles, priests, the educated say it's about) and a folk version (what everyone else actually does). The gap is fertile ground:

- Elite: "Marriage is a sacred lifelong bond." Folk: "Most peasants live together for years before formalizing, and divorce is a quiet matter."
- Elite: "We worship the seven gods." Folk: "We also leave milk for the house-spirit, and the village has its own saint the priests don't recognize."

Building both layers makes a culture immediately feel real.

## Internal diversity

A monolithic culture is a flag that worldbuilding has stopped early. Every culture contains:

- **Regional variation:** north vs. south, coastal vs. inland, urban vs. rural
- **Class variation:** noble customs, merchant customs, laborer customs, beggar customs
- **Generational variation:** what young people are doing that elders disapprove of
- **Religious variation:** orthodox, reformed, mystic, lapsed
- **Subcultures:** the soldier class, the scholar class, the criminal underclass — each with their own slang, ethics, dress

A useful exercise: for any cultural claim, ask *which subset of this culture would object?* If none, you've drawn it too flat.

## Cultures and other cultures

How does this culture see its neighbors? How do neighbors see it? Cultures define themselves *against* others:

- Stereotypes they hold (probably wrong, sometimes revealingly right)
- Loanwords from neighbor languages (what did they import, materially and conceptually?)
- Shared history — who fought whom, who traded with whom, whose princess married whose prince
- Diaspora — communities of this culture living abroad, often more conservative than the homeland

A culture only viewed from the inside looks like the inside of a sealed room. Show it from outside too.

## Common pitfalls

- **Culture as costume.** Generic European medieval with the serial numbers filed off, plus a "distinctive" hat or food. Not a culture; a wardrobe.
- **Hat culture.** One-trait cultures: "the warrior race," "the merchant race," "the music people." Real cultures contain warriors, merchants, *and* musicians, plus farmers, healers, drunks, and weirdos.
- **Anachronism by default.** Modern liberal values in a premodern setting often go unexamined. If the culture has them, *show why*. If it doesn't, render the alternative with respect — the people inside it are still people.
- **Romanticization or villainization.** A "perfect" non-European culture is as fake as a "savage" one. Every culture is internally contested, partially noble, partially monstrous.
- **Translating Earth cultures with a fantasy filter.** "Fantasy Mongols." "Fantasy Japan." Borrow and recombine; don't copy and rename.

## Originality moves

- **Find the unspoken rule.** What's the cultural thing everyone knows but never says? A topic adults change the subject around? That's where stories live.
- **Make values cost something.** A culture that prizes hospitality should sometimes go hungry because a guest came. Values without trade-offs are decoration.
- **Build the calendar.** A unique annual cycle of festivals, taboos, and observances generates plot hooks for years. (What happens *this* week? What happens at the new moon?)
- **Imagine a specific Tuesday.** Walk through one ordinary day for one ordinary person in this culture, hour by hour. Things you didn't know about the culture will surface.
- **Cross two unrelated influences.** A culture that's half Tibetan monastery and half Italian merchant republic. Half Aztec and half Quaker. The combination forces choices that pure imitation never does.

## Output format

```
# [Culture name]

## In a paragraph
Who they are, where they live, what makes them themselves.

## Origin & shaping forces
Geography, history, beliefs, neighbors that made them.

## Material life
Food, dress, shelter, key objects.

## Social structure
Family, class, gender, age, outsiders.

## Values & ethics
Highest virtue, cardinal sin, honor.

## Daily texture
Greetings, calendar, festivals, leisure.

## Conflict & justice
Disputes, crime, punishment, war.

## Internal diversity
Regions, classes, generations, subcultures.

## How others see them / how they see others

## Open questions
```

For very large cultures, split into a directory: `cultures/[name]/` with separate files for religion, language, history-as-they-remember-it, etc.

# Factions

A faction is any organized group with shared goals and the capacity to act on them. Kingdoms, guilds, churches, criminal syndicates, corporations, secret societies, revolutionary cells, mercenary companies, monastic orders. Factions are what turn lore into plot — they want things, they collide, they win and lose.

## The minimum viable faction

- **Name** (and informal nicknames, sometimes derogatory)
- **What they want** (concrete; "more power" is not concrete)
- **What they're willing to do** to get it
- **Who runs them** (a person, a council, a cycle of elections, a hereditary line)
- **What stands in their way** (other factions, internal divisions, scarce resources)
- **What would make them victorious** (state it explicitly; if you can't, the faction is shapeless)

These six are enough to use a faction in a story.

## The faction anatomy

When building a major faction, work through:

### Identity
- Name (formal, common, derogatory used by enemies)
- Symbol, colors, slogan, salute
- Founding story — who started it, when, why
- Core stated values vs. actual operating values (often diverge)

### Goals
- **Strategic goal** — the long-term vision (e.g., "restore the old kingdom")
- **Operational goals** — the medium-term moves (control these provinces, infiltrate that institution)
- **Tactical urgencies** — what they're trying to do *this season*
- **Secret goals** — what the leadership wants that the membership doesn't know

The three timescales should connect but rarely line up perfectly. The friction between strategic vision and tactical compromise drives faction-internal drama.

### Structure
- **Hierarchy:** flat, single-leader, council, dual-chamber, cellular?
- **Membership:** how do people join? Are they born in, recruited, initiated, conscripted?
- **Discipline:** how does the faction enforce conformity? Honor, contract, fear, faith, payment?
- **Communication:** how do orders move? How does information flow back up?
- **Succession:** what happens when the leader dies, falls, is removed?

### Resources
- **Manpower:** how many people, how loyal, how skilled?
- **Money:** where it comes from, where it goes
- **Material:** weapons, ships, factories, sacred relics, magical tools
- **Territory:** what they hold, openly or covertly
- **Reputation:** what people say about them; how that helps or hurts
- **Information:** what they know that others don't; what they're trying to find out

### Methods
- What tools does this faction reach for first? Diplomacy, money, intimidation, assassination, ritual, propaganda, open war?
- What lines do they refuse to cross? (Even cruelest factions usually have some.)
- What do they regret? (Past methods that backfired.)

### Internal politics
- **Factions within the faction.** Almost every real group has wings — hardliners, moderates, reformers, traditionalists. Build at least two.
- **Personalities at the top.** Who are the major figures, and what do they want personally that might cut against the faction line?
- **The next generation.** Who's rising? Who feels passed over?
- **Recent crises.** What's the faction worried about right now?

### External relations
- **Allies:** firm, contingent, secret. With what mutual interest?
- **Enemies:** mortal, opportunistic, ideological. Why?
- **Rivals:** factions with similar goals competing for the same prize.
- **Useful idiots:** groups they manipulate.
- **Sponsors / patrons:** powers above them, if any.

## The good antagonist faction

If a faction is the opposition, the same rules as character antagonists apply (see `references/worldbuilder/characters.md`):

- They're right about something
- Their worldview is internally coherent
- They have virtues
- They're vulnerable
- Their members are people, not avatars of an idea

A faction whose every member is uniformly evil is a flag that worldbuilding has stopped. Even the cruelest historical factions contained doubters, opportunists, true believers, and people who joined because their cousin was already in.

## Scaling: faction size & reach

Match structure to scale.

- **Tiny** (a dozen people, one location): no formal hierarchy needed; everyone knows everyone; one charismatic founder usually
- **Small** (dozens to hundreds): emerging hierarchy; ritual or contract to bind; one or two clear leaders
- **Medium** (hundreds to low thousands): formal structure; chapters or cells; bureaucratic friction begins
- **Large** (thousands+): substantial institutional machinery; written rules; specialized roles; internal politics matter as much as external

Bureaucracy isn't dull — it's where most of the actual drama of large factions plays out. Promotions, audits, factional alliances at headquarters, internal investigations, succession crises. A megacorp with no internal politics is missing 80% of itself.

## Faction conflicts

For each pair of factions in the world, ask:

- **What do they want from each other?** (Cooperation, surrender, conversion, annihilation?)
- **What history do they share?** (Past wars, alliances, betrayals, marriages?)
- **What stops them from open conflict?** (Mutual deterrence, third-party mediation, internal weakness, shared enemy?)
- **What would tip them over?** (Specific events that would trigger escalation.)

Faction conflicts are story engines. A world with three factions has three pairwise relations; six factions has fifteen. Don't try to map them all — pick the ones the story will touch and build those.

## Common pitfalls

- **The monolith.** Faction described as a single united actor. Real factions have internal divisions; surface at least one.
- **Generic evil empire / good rebels.** This is the laziest faction setup in fantasy. If you use it, undercut it — the empire has decent people; the rebels have brutal ones.
- **Goals too abstract.** "World domination," "spread chaos," "the natural order." Push to concrete: what does success look like next year?
- **No resource constraints.** Factions that always have enough soldiers, money, and information are inert. Scarcity creates choices.
- **Static factions.** Faction described as if it's been the same forever. Real factions evolve — leadership changes, doctrine shifts, new generations push different agendas.
- **Forgetting the rank-and-file.** Faction defined entirely by leadership. What does a typical mid-rank member's day look like? What do they actually believe?

## Originality moves

- **Cross genre conventions.** A merchant guild that operates like a monastic order. A revolutionary cell that runs like a corporation. A criminal syndicate organized as a family democracy.
- **Make the founding myth wrong.** The faction believes one origin story; the truth is different. Whoever knows the truth has leverage.
- **Make the membership perk weird.** People join for a non-obvious benefit — community, identity, protection, an obscure privilege, the chance to use a specific room in a specific building.
- **Make the symbol mean something specific.** Not "an eagle for power." A faction whose symbol is a half-eaten apple, with a precise reason.
- **Build the rival inside.** The most dangerous threat to most factions is internal. Plant a credible challenger to current leadership and let it run.
- **Make alliances awkward.** Allies who barely tolerate each other; enemies who quietly cooperate against a worse third party. Pure black-and-white alliances are rare.

## Output format

```
# [Faction name]

## In a line
One sentence: who they are and what they want.

## Founding
When, where, by whom, why.

## Strategic goal
The long view. Be specific.

## Operational moves
What they're doing this year/season.

## Structure & leadership
Hierarchy, current key figures.

## Resources
Manpower, money, material, territory, information.

## Methods
What they reach for first. What they won't do.

## Internal factions
Wings within the wing.

## External relations
Allies, enemies, rivals, dependents.

## Symbol & culture
What it feels like to be inside this faction.

## Current crisis
What they're worried about right now.

## Open questions
```

For very large factions, split into `factions/[name]/` with separate files for sub-organizations, key personalities, and ongoing operations.
